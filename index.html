When a heart stops, you are the first responder.

Heart disease is the world's leading cause of death. Many people who collapse could live, but the people standing next to them freeze, mistake a heart attack for "gas", or don't know CPR. PulseGuard trains you for the first 10 minutes, before the ambulance arrives.

CPR rhythm trainer

Hands-only CPR keeps blood moving to the brain while the heart can't pump on its own. Your compressions do the pumping job of the ventricles. Tap the chest (or press the space bar) and try to stay in the green zone of 100–120 per minute.

Heart lab: how your heart works, made simple

Think of your heart as a house with 4 rooms and two jobs. The right side sends tired blood to the lungs to pick up oxygen. The left side sends fresh blood to the whole body. Doors (valves) only open one way, and a tiny built-in spark keeps the beat.

The picture shows the heart as if you are facing someone, so their right side is on your left. Tap a room to learn about it.
